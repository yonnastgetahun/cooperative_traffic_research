# Research foundations (living notes)

## Established ideas
- **Wardrop's equilibrium:** Self-selected routes can settle into a user equilibrium that differs from a system optimum.
- **Price of anarchy:** Formalizes potential loss from decentralized self-interest.
- **Braess's paradox:** Network changes can worsen equilibrium travel times.
- **Traffic flow instability:** Driver reactions can propagate stop-and-go waves.
- **Cooperative control:** Vehicle control can mitigate waves in particular experiments; this does not validate phone-only routing.
- **Swarm intelligence / Physarum networks:** Inspiration for decentralized coordination, not evidence of smartphone effectiveness.
- **Mechanism design:** Voluntary cooperation needs incentives and fairness.
- **Induced demand:** Long-run traffic benefits can be offset by additional trips.
- **Multi-agent systems, distributed optimization, penetration rate:** Useful frameworks and experimental variables.

## Distinctions
Autonomy is not cooperation. Routing coordination is not vehicle-level control. An optimized simulated network is not evidence of deployability.

## Open questions
What is the credible navigation baseline? How many drivers must participate and comply? Who incurs extra delay? Can routes be coordinated without burdening residential streets? What traffic data are available for calibration?
