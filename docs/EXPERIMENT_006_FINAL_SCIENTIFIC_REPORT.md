# Experiment 006 — Frozen E versus independent A: final scientific findings

**Completed:** October 31, 2026.  
**Primary scientific decision: INCONCLUSIVE for network-mean superiority of clock-aligned E over independent nominal free-flow A.** The 200-second independent-seed paired confidence interval includes zero. **Joint efficiency/fairness GO criterion NOT MET**: the prespecified severe-driver-delay safeguard was violated. Preserve the unfavorable distributional result rather than retuning the experiment.

## Prespecified individual fairness and exception gates: high congestion

| Gate / observation | Prespecified rule | Measured | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Completion | All A/E trips complete, otherwise withhold GO pending censoring analysis | 100% in all 60 arms | **Pass** |
| Primary efficacy | 95% seed-level CI for A−E strictly positive | −1.912 to +2.398s | **Not established** |
| Mean seed-level p90 E−A | No more than +5s | **+1.7s** | **Pass prespecified tolerance**, not proof of equivalent tails |
| Fraction harmed by E | No more than 50% | **4,996/10,000 = 49.96%** | **Pass by narrow margin** |
| Fraction harmed by E >30 seconds | No more than 10% | **2,798/10,000 = 27.98%** | **FAIL** |
| Structured exceptions | No material E disadvantage | 0 recorded collisions, emergency stops, emergency braking in both arms across all scenarios | Pass for recorded SUMO fields only |

All matched high-congestion drivers: **4,706 (47.06%) shorter trips in E**, **4,996 (49.96%) longer**, **298 (2.98%) unchanged**. The mean of seed-level **p95 individual E−A harm** was **102.9 seconds**, with a descriptive 95% seed-level interval **[97.31,108.49]s**. This is the *within-driver contrast* upper-tail metric, NOT the p95 of absolute trip durations.

**Crucial finding:** Existing candidate route guardrails constrain **nominal/free-flow detour distance and time**, not the realized SUMO travel-time difference. They did not protect the 27.98% of drivers who experienced more than 30 seconds of additional realized delay. This is a demonstrated failure to meet the predeclared *realized-harm* criterion, not evidence of real-driver voluntary willingness.

The **formal primary decision remains INCONCLUSIVE** because its CI crosses zero. But the *joint cooperative-efficacy-and-fairness criterion is NOT MET*, independently reinforced by the failed severe-delay safeguard. Do not call this a GO.

## Who benefits? Matched changed- and unchanged-route decomposition

| Window | Status of vehicle's A/E route edge sequence | Matched drivers | Mean A−E travel time | Seed-level 95% CI | E harmed >30s |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| High 200s | **Changed route** | 5,703 | **−2.6192s** | [−5.2953,+0.0569] | 1,916 |
| High 200s | **Same route** | 4,297 | **+4.0413s** | [+2.1460,+5.9365] | 882 |
| Moderate 600s | Changed | 5,757 | −0.8330s | [−2.3218,+0.6558] | 555 |
| Moderate 600s | Same | 4,243 | +1.3628s | [+0.6211,+2.1045] | 183 |
| Lower 1200s | Changed | 5,193 | **−1.4235s** | [−1.8116,−1.0354] | 75 |
| Lower 1200s | Same | 4,807 | +0.6476s | [+0.4788,+0.8163] | 18 |

For 200s, drivers switched onto different routes by E averaged **2.62 seconds worse**, while drivers whose routes were unchanged averaged **4.04 seconds better**. This can nearly cancel in the network mean while leaving concentrated severe individual losses. However, **changed-vs-unchanged membership is determined after policy assignment**; these subgroup associations must not be interpreted as independently randomized treatment effects. Same-route effects are consistent with network-mediated spillovers, but specific causative bottlenecks remain unidentified.

## Structured safety/exception outcomes

The successful evaluator read original `statistics.xml` and `collisions.xml` files from **both arms of all 30 paired archives** (60 arm outputs). `safety.collisions`, `safety.emergencyStops`, `safety.emergencyBraking` and the collision-file event count were each **0 for A and E** across all regimes, with values present. No real-world vehicle safety conclusion follows.

## Decision and evidence boundaries

**Primary efficiency:** INCONCLUSIVE (200s A−E +0.2429s; CI includes zero).  
**Joint network-efficiency + fairness:** **NO-GO for claiming a successful E-over-A cooperative alternative under the prespecified joint criteria**, chiefly because high-congestion severe-driver-harm fraction 27.98% exceeds the 10% limit.  
**Historical status:** Experiment 004 D remains NO-GO; Experiment 005 clock-aligned E remains GO relative to D (not independently routed A).

This is not a universal refutation of cooperative traffic: only frozen E versus *original independent free-flow A* was compared, with all 1,000 routes assigned from announced demand in a synthetic grid. This experiment did not test modern independent traffic-aware routing, real driver compliance, participation fractions, departure coordination, grid scaling, or nonuniform topology.

## Next scientific question (not a new simulation authorization)

**How can collective routing decisions protect drivers against *realized* harm, rather than nominal/free-flow detours?** First use the original 30 Experiment 006 A/E paired archives to decompose severe realized delays (>30s) by route change, origin/destination grouping at sufficient sample sizes, nominal route geometry, waiting/queue exposure and assigned-edge concentration. Determine which mechanism(s) could plausibly produce a conditional or harm-bounded coordination policy. Do not immediately retune the routes or assume the current E can be rescued.

**One scientific conclusion:** Clock alignment remedied a real failure of the previous calibrated cooperative mechanism; **it did not yet make cooperative routing both collectively superior and individually fair**.
